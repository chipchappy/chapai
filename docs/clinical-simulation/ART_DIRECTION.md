# Patient Scene Art Direction

## Intent

The scene should feel like a calm, premium medical editorial illustration that becomes clinically urgent through patient state, not visual theatrics. It should remain recognizably Clarity: warm neutral surfaces, restrained clinical greens, legible dark text, and limited safety red or amber.

## Form

- Adult, anatomically plausible proportions
- Layered 2.5D perspective with bed, room, occlusion, shadow, and equipment depth
- Carefully shaded vectors rather than clip art, game characters, stock people, or uncanny generated faces
- Original device silhouettes with live text rendered programmatically
- Restrained facial expressions and movement
- Clinical dignity and minimal exposure

## Color

Skin findings use six baseline-aware palettes. Pallor, cyanosis, erythema, jaundice, moisture, and mottling must be assessed at appropriate regions rather than painted over the entire body. Mottling is irregular and distal; it must never resemble a repeating blanket print. Safety colors are reserved for alarms, bleeding, unsafe connections, and urgent state.

## Density

The scene may contain many devices, but the airway, patient face, major connection points, and monitor must remain readable. Use one cable trunk with short branches where possible. Persistent hotspots remain subtle until hover, focus, or selection. Avoid floating labels, decorative panels, glow effects, and unnecessary animation.

## Unit Identity

- Medical-surgical: lower equipment density and monitoring only when present
- Telemetry: cardiac monitoring and emergency equipment availability
- Step-down: continuous monitoring and respiratory support
- ICU: multiple access, pumps, drainage, monitoring, and escalation equipment
- Procedural: procedure light, airway rescue, suction, and sedation monitoring
- Psychiatric: calm, ligature-conscious, low-clutter, non-stigmatizing environment

## Review Gate

Every art change requires visual consistency, responsive, accessibility, provenance, and clinical-placement review. Qualified reviewers should specifically inspect oxygen interfaces, ECG placement, IV laterality, tube routing, drain position, skin findings across tones, bed angle, and patient dignity.
